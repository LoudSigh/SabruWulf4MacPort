# Sabre Wulf reference cross-check

The user pointed to [ArcadeGeek's SkoolKit Sabre Wulf index](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/), labeled there as a **work-in-progress** RAM disassembly. Useful navigable references include its [data map](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/maps/data.html), [routines map](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/maps/routines.html), [backgrounds](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/graphics/backgrounds.html), [graphics index](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/graphics/graphics.html), [game-state buffer](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/buffers/gbuffer.html) and [credits](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/reference/credits.html).

**Copyright boundary:** the pages credit Ultimate Play the Game and ArcadeGeek; the linked upstream repository reports no explicit reuse license. These pages are pointers for analysis, **not** permission to copy their disassembly, graphics, rendered room images or game data into this repository. The user additionally requires **no disassembly on GitHub**. Keep all source bytes, local listings and derived original layouts/images inside ignored `reverse_engineering/private/` only. Public analysis here contains numeric addresses, counts, hashes and independently worded conclusions.

## Quest/scoring leads requiring local source tests

The [1984 instructions transcription](https://www.zx-spectrum.cz/data/info/SabreWulf.txt) describes automatic pickup, four amulet pieces and a guarded exit. ArcadeGeek's work-in-progress listings label a [piece-placement routine](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/41557.html), [score addition](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/46505.html), [score display](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/46540.html), [completion](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/40810.html) and [game over](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/43626.html). These are **testable hypotheses**, not validated behavior for the user's snapshot or permission to copy code. Capture pre/post pickup states without a pickup key, all four piece indicators, guarded exit with three versus four pieces, 1UP/2UP score changes and a completion screen distinct from life-loss game over. Do not assume fixed piece locations, item point values or a universal score award. The instructions' E/R direction labels disagree with the independently measured **E up/R down** on this exact gameplay capture, so source input replays take precedence.
A bounded private PC trace has now confirmed that the externally labeled **41557** site is reached twice per new-game restart in two independently RAM-verified 800-frame paths. Each call changes sparse fields in **four 12-byte dynamic records** with valid and distinct room/sprite fields, but second-call rooms and two coordinates differ between paths. [Only aggregate measurements](../reverse_engineering/analysis/reference-quest-placement.json) are public. This verifies a four-record placement event on these two inputs, **not** that the objects are amulet pieces or that their locations, collection and victory rules match the external description. Keep actual record bytes/positions private until pickup effects are observed.
The private follow-up identifies per-record offsets **+0 sprite ID, +1 room index, +3/+4 X/Y**; offset +5 is written but unclassified. All **16/16** room writes match the corresponding world-layout lookup index read during that call, and **12/12** held-out records preserve the learned per-record sprite IDs. The routine reads the source RNG global once per invocation but no clock global directly. This is **not** a verified RNG→placement formula: the two equal-RNG calls also shared output, while the differing pair varied in room/position and entry registers. The lookup index's origin, quest identity and pickup rule still require controlled source experiments.
At the frame-790 playable checkpoint, neither of those two restarts puts the player in a record's room; the closest recorded rooms are five and four world-grid steps away. Later replay confirmed **one selected record** still active at frames 790 and 900; the other three were not checked. No counterfactual player teleport was run: changing room/X/Y alone would not reproduce the source's full cross-room state. First capture a naturally reached same-room active record or validate all state needed for a controlled transition.
Follow-up private reference replays kept one selected record active from frame 790 through 900. Holding right advanced 73 in-room pixels until frame 819; holding down advanced 22 until frame 802. Both had **900/900 full RAM parity**, no room transition, no injury/life write and no record contact. This is a starting-room traversal blocker, **not** an item disappearance or proof of a specific obstacle. A source-checked route around the barrier is needed before the automatic-pickup hypothesis can be tested.
An isolated static-bounds search proposed a two-turn east detour, but two source replays stopped at the first **other-actor contact/injury** before any room transition (contacts at 842/844, injuries at 855/846). Static path clearance is not a substitute for dynamic source safety; the candidate record remained active and uncontacted. No pickup or score result exists.
Two private replays with **simultaneous T and movement** instead deferred contact/injury (one had neither through frame 900; the other contacted at 884 and injured at 887), yet each advanced only 64 eastward pixels with no room crossing. Both keyboard-matrix paths matched the independent emulator's full RAM until their stopping frames. Do not interpret this as a sword hit or a source-backed pickup; it narrows the next obstacle to the route/geometry or another movement gate.
The observed east stall has a bounded static explanation: source velocity +32 in sixteenth-pixel units produced a two-pixel candidate east step, while the checked 14×22 background geometry had room for only one pixel. All **42/42** subsequent two-pixel probes were blocked despite 43 actor-update entries. An initial six-pixel east shift also obstructed the proposed north clearance; removing that shift yielded a source-verified, injury-free **north** transition at frame 827 (827/827 RAM frames). The record became one room farther away, so this is navigation evidence rather than pickup progress.

The source-backed ranges `0x6066–0x70BB`, contiguous background records `0x70BC–0x9691`, sprite pointer table `0xBF84–0xC10B`, and its bounded sprite records are now data in [byte-coverage.json](../reverse_engineering/analysis/byte-coverage.json). Screen bitmap/attributes are also known, totaling **29,178 classified data bytes** of 49,152 RAM bytes. The remaining **19,974** stay `unknown`; diagnostic instruction starts do not classify instruction lengths. [room-index.json](../reverse_engineering/analysis/room-index.json), [background-index.json](../reverse_engineering/analysis/background-index.json) and [sprite-index.json](../reverse_engineering/analysis/sprite-index.json) contain only numeric counts and hashes.

Two independently recovered Sabreman bitmap records (sprite IDs 16 and 21) were compared **privately** to the site's locally downloaded sample images. The simple 1-bit MSB-first bitmap shape matches their nonblack pixels exactly (336/336 and 352/352) after accounting for the image reader's vertical coordinate direction. A generic [SpriteMask decoder](../Sources/GameCore/SpriteMask.swift) and synthetic unit tests are in the shared Swift package; no bitmap payload or source PNG is in Git. The check does not validate the full sprite catalogue or original display attributes.
The native viewer can now **optionally** show local sprite samples 16 and 21 as thumbnails from a user-selected private folder. This is an inspection surface, not a sprite renderer or permission to distribute those images; playback and simulation remain independent of imported art.

The local [sprite index tool](../reverse_engineering/tools/SnapshotSpriteIndex.swift) can export all 196 pointer IDs and their 153 target records to an **ignored private atlas** after checking both snapshots agree. The shared [SpriteAtlas](../Sources/GameCore/SpriteAtlas.swift) verifies the pointer-table and record hashes, explicitly represents the empty sentinel and decodes the 152 nonempty bitmap masks. In the native viewer, import that atlas to browse any pointer ID and render its shape in newly drawn monochrome pixels; the private JSON and rendered source pixels are never bundled or tracked. This verifies byte-to-silhouette decoding, **not** original palette/compositing or which animation frames follow which events.
When the verified T-key replay supplies a player actor-state ID, the viewer can look up that index in the private atlas and draw its mask beside the recorded frame. All 100 recorded T-run IDs have nonempty bounded masks; that does not prove how the original game layered sprites, assigned colors, or selected frames outside this capture.
The [player-screen verifier](../reverse_engineering/tools/VerifyActorScreen.swift) now independently compares bottom-anchored, vertically reversed player masks against the reference's actual ULA bitmap/attributes across five 100-frame Q/W/E/R/T runs. Its [numeric evidence](../reverse_engineering/analysis/actor-screen.json) has **489/500 fully matching actor rectangles** and **173,348/173,472 matching pixels**. Only W frames 43–53 differ (124 white/off pixels, including 64 bitmap-bit differences); whether this is overlap, redraw phase or an attribute change remains unknown. This establishes observed player placement and several recorded animations, **not** general layering or enemy/projectile rendering. The native viewer's local white actor overlay is hideable and must not substitute for native state or combat.

The local [room index tool](../reverse_engineering/tools/SnapshotRoomIndex.swift) extracts the **41 distinct background records** behind the 919 placements: each record holds 1-bit bitmap rows followed by a bounded 8×8 attribute grid. The **9,686 recovered bytes** have the same aggregate hash in both snapshots, recorded in [background-index.json](../reverse_engineering/analysis/background-index.json). The shared [BackgroundAtlas](../Sources/GameCore/BackgroundAtlas.swift) checks that hash and validates every imported placement before the viewer draws locally generated monochrome shapes. A separately labeled optional [48K attribute decoder](../Sources/GameCore/SpectrumAttribute.swift) applies the standard ink/paper/bright bits to the local preview; FLASH cadence, transparency, draw order and exact full-scene composition have **not** been verified.
Private E/R renders show that E decreases screen Y (visible up) and R increases screen Y (visible down). The room renderer also uses the **source Y directly** for backgrounds. The native [coordinate mapping](../Sources/GameCore/SpectrumCoordinates.swift) therefore places backgrounds at `sourceY` and actor markers at their reported Y; its earlier bottom-origin conversion was incorrect.
Private inspection of the original room drawing routine confirms that it complements background bitmap bits before writing them to the display. When the locally decoded bitmap/attribute records are placed in room-template order at direct X/Y and the bits are complemented, the captured gameplay screen agrees at **29,056/29,056 covered RGB pixels**. Without bit complementation the same direct placement matched **0/29,056**. Reference-relative replay additionally matches **28,544/28,544** covered pixels in north-exit room 152 (template 6) and **30,720/30,720** in west-exit room 151 (template 14). The latter replay matches **260/260 complete RAM frames** against the backup emulator. These checks establish only the covered static regions of three templates; uncovered pixels, other rooms, dynamic sprites and FLASH cadence remain unverified. See the numeric-only [background index](../reverse_engineering/analysis/background-index.json) and [local reproduction steps](./local-reverse-engineering.md).

## Reproduce and inspect privately

Run from the repository root. This tool uses the locally installed Carbon Neural emulator's `SpeccyCore` parser without copying its source here:

```sh
SPECCY_CORE_DIR='/Volumes/July2025inclOct2022/Visual Studio Code Backups/Carbon Neural/2025-09-13_22-10-25/CarbonNeural_portable_2025-09-13_22-10-25/Sources/SpeccyCore'
OUT="$(mktemp -d)"
swiftc -O -parse-as-library "$SPECCY_CORE_DIR"/*.swift reverse_engineering/tools/SnapshotRoomIndex.swift -o "$OUT/SnapshotRoomIndex"
"$OUT/SnapshotRoomIndex" --self-test
"$OUT/SnapshotRoomIndex" SNAPSHOTS/Snapshot.z80 SNAPSHOTS/Snapshot_GamePlay.z80 --private-map
open reverse_engineering/private/snapshot-803e4197989c-world.html
```

The opened **private** local HTML shows 256 world positions colored by room-template ID (generic colors and numbers, not original graphics). The same private tool additionally writes a validated version-2 JSON world with all 48 room templates and measured width/height for each background to `reverse_engineering/private/snapshot-803e4197989c-world-v2.json`. The native macOS/iOS/visionOS apps can import that file into a **read-only** world explorer (see [build.md](build.md)); no original map data is bundled. Previously exported version-1 files remain importable for topology browsing, but cannot answer bounding-box queries. Both outputs and all snapshot bytes stay ignored by Git. In the captured gameplay room, a 24-by-24 background at X=32/Y=112 is consistent with the reference player stopping at X=56 when holding Q (the next leftward position overlaps its bounds). Another background at X=48/Y=40 with height 24 is consistent with E stopping at Y=87 (Y=85 overlaps while Y=86 is clear; the movement step can skip it). The measured test treats actor X as its left edge and Y as its bottom edge, including 8-bit arithmetic wraparound. The shared `WorldReference.overlapsBackgroundBounds` query and independent-axis `resolveBackgroundBounds` cover only static rectangles, without asserting dynamic actor or boundary-transition fidelity. They are not connected to the playable prototype; exact collision responses in all rooms remain unverified. This reference cross-check advances *static* map recovery; it does not establish tape revision equality or continuous input-driven behavior.
Version-2 native imports now verify four reconstructed source hashes: layout, room pointer table, room records and a width/height-inclusive 6,433-byte placement digest. The hashes are source-free metadata in [room-index.json](../reverse_engineering/analysis/room-index.json). A forged layout or altered background bound can no longer look like a successful verified import; version-1 imports are labeled legacy structural references only.

### Provisional westward path

The public [west-exit-schedule.json](../reverse_engineering/analysis/west-exit-schedule.json) uses W on frames 20–29, E on 30–99, no input through frame 179, then Q on 180–255. The legacy absolute-boundary CPU replay moves from room **152** into room **151** at frame **226**, freezes for seven frames and rebases to X=239/Y=126 on frame **233**. Native static movement matches room/X/Y for all **256** frames of *that* replay. With the original emulator's per-call **relative** frame boundaries, the same CPU and fresh keyboard input now match **all 256 full 48K RAM frames** of `stepFrame()`, including RNG, enemy route and the player entering damage state at frame 164. In the old absolute replay, the first RNG, enemy, player-state and position differences occur at frames 101, 102, 163 and 183, respectively. The causal factor was frame-boundary drift, not a faulty keyboard. This west-exit path remains **provisional as native gameplay**: the static-only Swift movement preview does not simulate the enemy encounter and cannot follow the reference-timed player through the west exit. A longer continuous-Q probe also encounters moving actors; no pauses are hard-coded.

`SnapshotDivergence.swift --trace` produces a **private** 256-frame numeric comparison of one entity slot and both player positions. The old absolute comparison shows the diverging routes; `--trace --reference-timing --require-ram-parity` shows aligned routes with **256/256 RAM-frame equality** and errors if that threshold fails. The app requires a matching replay *and timing mode* to import either trace. Neither contains original sprite pixels or implements enemy AI.

### Bounded attack-state observation

The source-free [T-key schedule](../reverse_engineering/analysis/fire-observation-schedule.json) presses T during zero-based frames 20–39. The frame-aligned private replay matches the unmodified emulator's **100/100 complete RAM and screen hashes**. The player remains at room 168, X=57/Y=112; its numeric actor-state field changes from 21 (frame 20) to 42 (21), 45 (23), 40 (27), 41 (31), 47 (35), then 20 (41, after release). These are state values, **not verified sprite animation frames or combat outcomes**. The native movement preview still rejects fire explicitly; the read-only viewer can scrub this recorded state without bundling original pixels.

Two further [source-free schedules](../reverse_engineering/analysis/fire-before-contact-schedule.json) (W/E then T) and [no-T control](../reverse_engineering/analysis/no-fire-encounter-schedule.json) capture **190 frames each** with full RAM/screen-hash agreement against the unmodified emulator. The first player-state difference is frame 146, enemy-state difference frame 156 and enemy-position difference frame 159. At frame 163, entity slot 12 is at X=86/Y=135 with T and X=104/Y=135 without; without T the player enters a damage state at frame 164, but with T it remains at X=121/Y=126. An [unrelated-A-key timing control](../reverse_engineering/analysis/unrelated-a-control-schedule.json) also matches 190/190 frames and shifts the enemy's position at frame 159, delaying damage until frame 170—but unlike T, it does not change enemy state at frame 156 or prevent damage through frame 190. [Numeric checkpoints](../reverse_engineering/analysis/reference-fire-encounter.json) and ignored traces allow private comparison. Later source-choice tracing identifies that **first** enemy difference: RNG inputs 227/55/28 select signed X velocities **−48/+96/+48** from their low three bits; the source updates the kind based on the **selected velocity sign**, not RNG bit 7. This is repeatable input-dependent motion, **not evidence of a sword hit**, score increment or general enemy death.
