## Quest/scoring leads requiring local source tests

The [1984 instructions transcription](https://www.zx-spectrum.cz/data/info/SabreWulf.txt) describes automatic pickup, four amulet pieces and a guarded exit. ArcadeGeek's work-in-progress listings label a [piece-placement routine](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/41557.html), [score addition](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/46505.html), [score display](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/46540.html), [completion](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/40810.html) and [game over](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/43626.html). These are **testable hypotheses**, not validated behavior for the user's snapshot or permission to copy code. Capture pre/post pickup states without a pickup key, all four piece indicators, guarded exit with three versus four pieces, 1UP/2UP score changes and a completion screen distinct from life-loss game over. Do not assume fixed piece locations, item point values or a universal score award. The instructions' E/R direction labels disagree with the independently measured **E up/R down** on this exact gameplay capture, so source input replays take precedence.
The [routines map](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/maps/routines.html) and [full memory-map index](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/maps/all.html) do **not** label a collectible-contact or pickup handler. Nearby labels such as [Remove Entity](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/47349.html) do not by themselves connect the four placed records to collection, score or victory. Avoid making that inference without a source contact/removal experiment.
A bounded private PC trace has now confirmed that the externally labeled **41557** site is reached twice per new-game restart in two independently RAM-verified 800-frame paths. Each call changes sparse fields in **four 12-byte dynamic records** with valid and distinct room/sprite fields, but second-call rooms and two coordinates differ between paths. [Only aggregate measurements](../reverse_engineering/analysis/reference-quest-placement.json) are public. This verifies a four-record placement event on these two inputs, **not** that the objects are amulet pieces or that their locations, collection and victory rules match the external description. Keep actual record bytes/positions private until pickup effects are observed.
The private follow-up identifies per-record offsets **+0 sprite ID, +1 room index, +3/+4 X/Y**; offset +5 is written but unclassified. All **16/16** room writes match the corresponding world-layout lookup index read during that call, and **12/12** held-out records preserve the learned per-record sprite IDs. The routine reads the source RNG global once per invocation but no clock global directly. This is **not** a verified RNG→placement formula: the two equal-RNG calls also shared output, while the differing pair varied in room/position and entry registers. The lookup index's origin, quest identity and pickup rule still require controlled source experiments.
Separately, the external [score-addition label](https://skoolkit.arcadegeek.co.uk/ultimate/sabrewulf/dec/asm/46505.html) now has a **private source-routine arithmetic check**: 25/25 executed calls across four schedules match independently authored packed-BCD updates to the selected three-byte player score buffer. All observed calls select 1UP. This [numeric evidence](../reverse_engineering/analysis/reference-score-arithmetic.json) says nothing about item point awards, source 2UP parity, why the routine was called, or how score glyphs are rendered.
At the frame-790 playable checkpoint, neither of those two restarts puts the player in a record's room; the closest recorded rooms are five and four world-grid steps away. Later replay confirmed **one selected record** still active at frames 790 and 900; the other three were not checked. No counterfactual player teleport was run: changing room/X/Y alone would not reproduce the source's full cross-room state. First capture a naturally reached same-room active record or validate all state needed for a controlled transition.

Both had **900/900 full RAM parity**, no room transition, no injury/life write and no record contact. This is a starting-room traversal blocker, **not** an item disappearance or proof of a specific obstacle. A source-checked route around the barrier is needed before the automatic-pickup hypothesis can be tested.

Static path clearance is not a substitute for dynamic source safety; the candidate record remained active and uncontacted. No pickup or score result exists.

Reuse a directed graph edge only after an actual source crossing from that entry pose. The remaining color-only mismatches do **not** prove an attribute-writing or draw-order rule. This establishes observed bitmap placement, **not** general layering or enemy/projectile rendering. The native viewer's local white actor overlay is hideable and must not substitute for native state or combat.
An optional, 100/100 RAM-parity [bounded attribute-write probe](../reverse_engineering/analysis/reference-actor-attribute-writes.json) observes 94 writes to nearby tiles during frames 40–55, including 22 changed writes to the two affected player-row cells across the 11 overlap frames. Detailed addresses, values and instruction traces remain private.

The other actor appears on eight alternating frames, and both contexts change the affected player cells 11 times in the overlap window. This narrows color provenance **without** establishing a general actor-update cadence, a color lookup rule or draw order.
For observation in the native app, the private [W overlap replay/actor trace](../reverse_engineering/analysis/reference-overlap-viewer.json) provides 100 source-verified frame pairs with player bitmap IDs and an active same-room slot-18 actor in all 11 overlap frames. Importing both plus the private sprite atlas draws only that measured two-mask XOR as a **monochrome recorded silhouette**, not source colors, autonomous AI or general dynamic compositing.

The local [room index tool](../reverse_engineering/tools/SnapshotRoomIndex.swift) extracts the **41 distinct background records** behind the 919 placements: each record holds 1-bit bitmap rows followed by a bounded 8×8 attribute grid.
